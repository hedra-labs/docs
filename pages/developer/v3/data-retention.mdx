Hedra keeps the files a job reads and writes for a fixed period, and then
erases them along with the job's prompt and logs. Download each output to your
own storage before it expires. To erase a job's data sooner, for example when
one of your users asks you to delete their photo or recording, delete the job.

## Download outputs before `expires_at`

In `GET /v3/jobs/{job_id}` and in webhook payloads, each image, video and audio
output includes `expires_at`, the deadline to download the output. By default,
`expires_at` is 24 hours after you submit the job.

```json
{
  "status": "COMPLETED",
  "asset_id": "asset_9b41…",
  "url": "https://…",
  "expires_at": "2026-10-09T17:42:06.318204Z",
  "content_type": "video/mp4",
  "width": 1280,
  "height": 720,
  "duration_ms": 5000
}
```

- `url` works for up to an hour. Every response from `GET /v3/jobs/{job_id}`,
  and every webhook delivery attempt, includes a newly signed `url`, so request
  the job again when you need a working `url`.
- To use the output as an input to another job, pass its `asset_id` before
  `expires_at`. See [Chain generations](/pages/developer/v3/chain-generations).

## When Hedra erases a job's data

By default, about four days after you submit a job that produces an image,
video or audio file, three days after its outputs' `expires_at`, Hedra erases:

- the job's outputs;
- the files the job read from your `POST /v3/files` uploads;
- the job's prompt;
- the job's logs, which `GET /v3/jobs/{job_id}/logs` returns.

From then on, `GET /v3/jobs/{job_id}` reports each output's `status` as
`EXPIRED`, with null `url` and `asset_id`, and no prompt, and
`GET /v3/jobs/{job_id}/logs` returns no events. Hedra erases every job's logs
after 30 days at the latest, and Hedra's operational logs and workflow
histories, which can contain job data, expire within 30 days.

Hedra keeps a usage record of the job: `GET /v3/jobs` still lists it with its
model, status and submission time, and `GET /v3/jobs/{job_id}` still reports its
cost and each output's dimensions and duration.

## Delete a job now

`DELETE /v3/jobs/{job_id}` erases a finished job's outputs, the files it read
from your uploads, its prompt and its logs before it returns `204`. Hedra then
keeps the job's usage record, as it does for an expired job.

```bash
curl -X DELETE https://api.hedra.com/v3/jobs/$JOB_ID \
  -H "Authorization: Key $HEDRA_KEY"
```

- The key needs the `jobs:write` scope.
- Delete a job once its `status` is `COMPLETED` or `FAILED`. While the job
  runs, or while another job that reads its output by `asset_id` runs, the
  request returns `409` with a `Retry-After` header.
- For a voice clone job, the request returns `400`.
- If the request returns `503`, send it again to finish the erasure. Deleting a
  job that Hedra has already erased returns `204`.
- Hedra erases an input that you passed by `asset_id` with the job that
  produced it.

## Choose a retention period for an enterprise workspace

An enterprise workspace can have its own retention period, from 6 hours to 30
days, in place of the default 24 hours.
[Talk to us](https://www.hedra.com/enterprise) to set one. The period applies to
the image, video and audio files of every job in the workspace: its outputs and
the files it read from your uploads. Each output's `expires_at` is then the
job's submission time plus the period, and Hedra erases the job's data within an
hour after that time.
